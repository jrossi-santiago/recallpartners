# WellFIT — cancelled member reactivation

Built for WellFIT Private Fitness & Rehab. Three emails over five days, matching
texts if you message members, and what to say when they reply.

Everything in `{{double braces}}` gets swapped per person. Everything else is
meant to be sent as written, from Patty's own address, in plain text — no
logo, no header image, no newsletter template. The whole point is that it
should not look like a blast, because a blast is what these people have been
ignoring.

---

## Before you send: split the list

Do not send one message to everyone. Four groups, and only the first three get
this sequence:

| Group | Who's in it | What changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A — recently gone** | Stopped in the last 6 months | Send as written. Name the month they stopped. |
| **B — a while back** | 6–18 months | Send as written. Drop the month, say "last year". |
| **C — long gone** | 18+ months | Email 1 only, then stop unless they reply. |
| **D — never started** | Consults and enquiries who never booked a first session | Different message entirely. Not this one. |

Pull anyone out who moved away, who left on bad terms, who asked not to be
contacted, or who you know has had something serious happen. That list is
usually shorter than owners expect, and going through it by hand is twenty
minutes well spent.

## Email 1 — Day 1 (Tuesday)

**Subject:** quick question, {{FirstName}}

```
{{FirstName}} — it's Patty at WellFIT.

You trained with us until {{Month}} and then we lost you. I've been
going back through the people who stopped, and yours was one of the
names I kept stopping on.

I'm not writing to sell you anything. I want to know what happened —
did the schedule stop working, did something start hurting, or did
life just get loud for a while?

One sentence back is plenty.

— Patty
WellFIT Private Fitness & Rehab
(210) 843-2995
```

**Why it's built this way.** No offer, no discount, no booking link. The only
thing this email asks for is a reply, and a reply is a low enough bar that
people clear it. It also gets you the one piece of information that decides
everything after it — why they actually left. An owner who knows that can make
a better offer on Thursday than any template can guess at on Tuesday.

Send this one on its own. Resist adding "P.S. we also have a special on…" — the
P.S. is what turns it back into marketing.

## Email 2 — Day 3 (Thursday)

**Subject:** an easier way back in

```
{{FirstName}} — following up on Tuesday.

Most people who stop training didn't stop caring about it. Something
changed — a shoulder, a job, a couple of months where the gym was the
first thing to go — and then coming back starts to feel like it needs
a big run-up. New plan, new schedule, getting back to where you were.

So here's the version with no run-up.

Come in for a 30-minute re-check with me. We look at where you are
now, not where you were when you left — how you're moving, what's
been bothering you, what's realistic with the schedule you actually
have. You walk out with a plan on paper.

No charge. Nothing sold at the end of it. No commitment to start back up.

If you decide afterwards that you want to train again, I'll walk you
through the options then. If you don't, you got a free assessment out
of it and I got to see you.

Reply with a day that works and I'll hold the time.

— Patty
```

**Why a re-check and not a discount.** Discounts answer a price objection.
Almost nobody quits a private gym over price — they quit over a hurt shoulder,
a new job, or three months of not going and then feeling too far behind to walk
back in. A discount doesn't touch any of that, and it teaches your list to wait
for the next one. A free re-check answers the actual objection: it makes the
first step small, it puts them in front of you, and it's worth something on its
own even to someone who never buys again.

If you want a paid step behind it, make it a defined restart — four weeks,
twice a week, one price, ends on a date — and only offer it on the re-check,
face to face. Don't put it in the email.

## Email 3 — Day 5 (Saturday morning)

**Subject:** last one from me

```
{{FirstName}} — last note, I promise.

The free re-check offer stands through {{Date}}. Thirty minutes, no
charge, nothing sold at the end.

And if now isn't the time, just tell me when. "Ask me in the fall" is
a real answer — I'll write it down and leave you alone until then.

Either way the door's open. You know where we are.

— Patty
1919 Lockhill Selma Rd, Suite 101
(210) 843-2995
```

**The "tell me when" line is the one that earns its keep.** It gives people who
aren't ready something to say other than nothing, and it hands you a follow-up
date you didn't have before. Keep a note of every one you get back and actually
honour it — a member who says "September" and hears from you in September
converts far better than a stranger.

## The same three, as texts

Only if you already text members and they'd recognise the number. A text from a
number they don't know reads as spam no matter how it's written.

**Day 1**
```
Hi {{FirstName}} — Patty from WellFIT. No pitch, I promise: I'm going
back through the people who stopped training with us and wanted to ask
what happened. Schedule, an injury, or just life? Reply here.
```

**Day 3**
```
{{FirstName}} — offering you a free 30-min re-check with me. We look at
where you're at now, you leave with a plan, nothing gets sold. Want a
day this week? — Patty, WellFIT
```

**Day 5**
```
{{FirstName}} — closing this out {{Date}}. Free 30-min re-check if you
want it, or tell me a month to check back and I'll leave it alone until
then. — Patty, WellFIT. Reply STOP to opt out.
```

Texts go out 10am–6pm, never Sunday morning. Every campaign text needs a way to
opt out; every opt-out gets honoured immediately and permanently.

## When they reply

Replies are the whole point, so plan for them. Short answers, sent the same day,
from you.

**"I got injured / I've been in pain."**
> That's exactly what the re-check is for — half of what I do here is training
> around something. Come in and let me see it before you decide anything.

**"I couldn't afford it."**
> Understood, and the re-check is free regardless. If you do want to start back
> up, there are lighter ways in than what you were doing before — semi-private
> is the usual one. Worth thirty minutes to look at.

**"I've been embarrassed how long it's been."**
> {{FirstName}}, I promise you I have seen every version of this. Nobody here is
> counting. Come in, we start from today.

**"I've been going somewhere else."**
> Good — genuinely, as long as you're training. If it ever stops working for
> you, you know where I am.
> *(Then take them off the list. Chasing this one costs you goodwill.)*

**"Ask me in {{Month}}."**
> Done — {{Month}} it is. I'll leave you alone until then.
> *(Write the date down. Actually follow up on it.)*

**No reply at all.** Stop after email 3. Someone who ignored three notes isn't
persuaded by a fourth; they're persuaded by hearing from you again in six
months, when something in their life has changed.

---

## How to judge it

Email 1's job is replies, not bookings — measure it that way, or you'll call a
working campaign a failure on day two. Bookings come off email 2 and 3, and off
the conversations email 1 starts.

Two things worth writing down before you send, so you can tell afterwards
whether it did anything: how many people you sent to, and how many sessions
those people booked in the 30 days after. The second number is the one that
matters, and it's the one nobody records.
